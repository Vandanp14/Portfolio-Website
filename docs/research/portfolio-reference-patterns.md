# Portfolio reference patterns

Research date: 2026-10-18. Scope: public homepage structure and visible interactions.

## Apple — product-led editorial

- Current homepage leads with a single product announcement and a short phrase, then gives two direct actions and a product image before moving into the next distinct story.
- The page changes visual treatment between stories while retaining a consistent, low-noise global navigation. Each story can be understood without reading a long paragraph.
- Transfer: give the portfolio one clear point of view, lead each project with its strongest outcome, and alternate visual emphasis so projects feel authored rather than templated.
- Avoid: product-launch hyperbole and copying Apple's color, imagery, or product tiles.

## Uber — utility and clear next steps

- The localized homepage puts its primary task (“Go anywhere”) and the pickup/drop-off fields at the top, then groups other use cases under direct section labels.
- The site repeats major actions at useful points and uses familiar navigation and footer groupings to make a large information space navigable.
- Transfer: make the portfolio's routes, contact action, résumé, and current role easy to find, while keeping the first viewport focused on the primary purpose: understanding Vandan's work.
- Avoid: task form UI, dense offering catalogs, and Uber's black/green brand treatment.

## Cluely — show the product early

- The homepage pairs a direct product promise with an interface demonstration near the top, then explains the product in short, sequenced sections with interface evidence.
- Specific interaction details make the feature story legible without relying only on abstract claims.
- Transfer: use real project screenshots and concrete engineering outcomes as proof near the beginning; frame each case study around a user problem and what shipped.
- Avoid: imitating its AI assistant chrome, claims, gradients, or any visual identity.

## Ranked transferable patterns

1. **Evidence before claims** — surface project visuals and verifiable outcomes in the initial scroll, drawing from Apple and Cluely.
2. **A clear action hierarchy** — keep work, résumé, and contact visible in navigation, borrowing Uber's task clarity.
3. **One story per section** — separate work into distinct editorial beats rather than repeating interchangeable cards.
4. **Concrete and concise copy** — use project outcomes and role context instead of broad self-description.
5. **Responsive wayfinding** — preserve direct navigation and touch-size controls across viewport sizes.
